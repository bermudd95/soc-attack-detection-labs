# Phishing Awareness Guide

## Recognize common warning signs

- **Unexpected requests:** Be cautious of messages asking you to reset a password, verify an account, review a payment, or open an unexpected document.
- **Sender mismatch:** Check the complete sender address, not only the display name. A familiar brand name does not prove the sender is legitimate.
- **Urgency or threats:** Messages that demand immediate action or threaten account suspension may be trying to prevent careful review.
- **Unexpected links:** Inspect the destination before opening it. Be especially cautious of shortened, misspelled, or unrelated domains.
- **Unexpected attachments:** Do not open files you were not expecting, especially when the message pressures you to act quickly.
- **Requests for credentials or MFA codes:** Do not provide passwords, one-time passcodes, or approve unexpected authentication prompts through an email link.

## What to do with a suspicious message

1. Do not click links, open attachments, reply, or provide credentials.
2. Use the organization's approved phishing-reporting button or process.
3. If no reporting button is available, contact the IT or security team through a known, trusted channel.
4. Keep the original message intact when possible so analysts can review its headers and metadata.
5. Follow instructions from the security team. Do not independently delete or forward the message if your organization's reporting process requires it to remain available.

## If you already interacted with the message

- Report what happened promptly and accurately: link clicked, attachment opened, credentials entered, or MFA prompt approved.
- If you entered a password, contact the organization's IT/security team immediately and follow its credential-reset instructions.
- Do not attempt to investigate or remove malware yourself unless directed by authorized support personnel.
- Report unexpected MFA prompts or suspicious account activity.

## Verify requests safely

If a message claims to come from a company, manager, or internal department, verify the request through a known contact method or a trusted internal portal. Do not use contact details or links supplied only in the suspicious message.

## Key point

Pause, verify, and report. Prompt reporting helps security teams determine who received the message, whether anyone interacted with it, and what response is appropriate.
